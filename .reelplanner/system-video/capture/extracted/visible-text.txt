# reelplanning — system spec

_Kept current by the agent after every accepted walkthrough. Names come from `glossary.md`; parts and edges from `system.json`. Prose here, data there._

## Purpose

Turns an implementation plan into a short narrated video so an engineer can review it by watching and reacting, then feeds what they mark, comment and decide back into the plan and the video itself — for the people building reelplanning, reviewing plans written by an agent.

The name says it: reelplanning sounds like "real planning". A plan as long text is too hard to take in, and gets skimmed and approved; its video, with the guide under it for the detail, is what makes planning real (D-304). Each plan goes round one lifecycle: the plan, its plan video and guide, your review in the player, your answers recorded as decisions, the build, its walkthrough video and guide, your review of that, and the system video brought up to date.

## Install

reelplanning is installed, so its system video shows installing it, with the real commands run (D-303). Per machine: the tooling with `npm i -g` from the GitHub repo (it is not on npm yet), which puts `reelplanning` and `reel` on the PATH; `reelplanning setup`, which installs what the pipeline shells out to (ffmpeg, a headless Chrome, Kokoro TTS, whisper.cpp) and HyperFrames' skills, skipping what is there; and the plan-to-video skill into each agent with `npx skills add … --skill plan-to-video -g`. Per repo: `reel init . --agent claude|codex|none` writes `.reelplanning/` (the spec, the parts, the glossary, the decision log, the theme, and `config.json` with the agent's headless command). It is tested with Claude Code, has basic support for Codex, and the other agents are open work (`docs/agents.md`). To start, ask the agent for a plan, or hand it one: `/plan-to-video <plan.md>` in Claude Code, `$plan-to-video` in Codex.

## Parts

- **The plan-to-video skill** — turns a plan.md into STORYBOARD.md/SCRIPT.md and, via HyperFrames, the frames, plus the detail pages its beats open (a page for what the video cannot hold, started with `reelplanning detail new`), each with the thing it explains marked in its frame (`data-detail`). If the sentence the video would say instead is enough, there is no detail. Every frame follows the style guide's seven frame rules (§5, a frame a newcomer can read): show the thing, never a stand-in; one reading order; each name on its thing; a question that says what you decide; nothing covering content; readable at a glance; pleasing. `frame-lint` fails the two a program can see, empty bars standing where words go and anything in the room the Open tab needs. Before a video reaches its reviewer, fresh eyes look at it: every new video, and every rebuild of an older one. `reelplanning fresh-eyes` writes a brief for two fresh agents, a newcomer and a designer, each with a picture of every scene at rest taken through the review page and nothing of the plan; the newcomer lists what it could not follow, the designer what breaks the frame rules. Every finding is answered under it before the page opens (fixed, a meaning, or kept with the reason), and `verify` stops the build on one with no answer; at most three rounds, and what the author kept after the third is said on the page's Before you watch and in the notification. A phrase fresh eyes flagged and the author kept gets a meaning: a row under the glossary's Other words, or the storyboard's `terms:`.
- **narrate** — voices the script one line at a time, and on a rebuild only the lines whose words, voice or speed changed; every other line keeps its recording. `reelplanning build` runs it, then finish-project and `verify`, as one command, and ends with the video's length against its budget (3–5 minutes; the system video 5–8).
- **finish-project** — the assembly tail (captions, index, transitions, plan map, plan-diff) that runs after frame content changes. It ends with the details check: a missing detail page, one that loads from the network, one that errors, or a frame that marks a detail its scene does not have stops the build.
- **plan-diff** — content-hash diffs a rebuilt video against the last committed one, so only what actually changed needs rewatching.
- **The review player** — `<reelplanning-player>`, where a reviewer watches, marks, comments and decides; its Plan switch (L) shows the plan's own text beside the video. It stops at each question (two to four options, or pick all that apply; any answer can carry a note), and the reviewer answers by clicking the frame's own option cards; what the frame can't take (own words, a note, Accept / Flag) goes in the answer band, in the lowest eighth every frame leaves empty for it (under the video, for a video made before that). The video never changes size. A question not answered stops the video each time it is reached; played back past an answered one, it shows again, answered, to change. After a quick check, the video waits 10 s, "Back to where this was explained" replays what it tests, and "Expected something else?" sends the reviewer's words to the agent as a comment. In a walkthrough, each choice that pauses carries Accept / Flag on its own card, and the rest are one list at the end, each with a Flag: Go on takes the others as listed, not judged; the header's Plan–Built switch jumps between a step's plan scene and the scene that runs it, and the open question ("Seeing it run, anything you'd change?") is asked again at Finish. A thing the frame marks for a part of the guide shows itself quietly (D-266): nothing while the video plays, a faint arrow when paused, and on hover a thin ring and a small grey "More in the guide ↓"; a click (or O) pauses the video and opens that part, and closing puts the video back as it was. Where nothing is marked, the Open chip in the frame's corner opens it. The Terms open in the side panel, beside the video; a word or phrase with a meaning is underlined in the captions, and a click shows it. Paused on any scene, **Ask about this** (Q, or a plain caption word clicked) opens in the side panel for a question in your own words: on a hosted page Claude answers from the plan, the glossary and the scene, and says where the answer came from; on your own machine the agent session waiting on the page answers, and with none waiting the question goes with the review, to be answered in the next version. Every question is kept in the review. Before the first play, Before you watch names the videos to watch first and what fresh eyes left as it is. Rewinds and slow-downs are sent as "hard to follow". After a rebuild it plays just the changes, with one toggle for the whole video. Finish ends a review: **Approve** (on a walkthrough, the build is then accepted) or **Request changes**. An explainer's Finish has three ends instead: **Done** (offered first), **Explain more** or **Plan this**; the last two each say what they would mean for this video, suggestions made at build time from its scenes, its long sources and the open threads `explain.md` names, and refined by what the viewer did while watching (a scene rewound or commented on, a question the page could not answer, a quick check missed, a word looked up, the commits since). The viewer picks one, edits it, or writes their own, under "What do you want next?", and the panel says that nothing goes into the decision log.
- **The guide** — the page under every video (D-264, D-265), built by `reelplanning guide` from `plan.md` (after the build, also `walkthrough.md` and the real runs in `runs/`), never committed. Scroll down from the video and the video goes on in a small player in the corner. It holds what the video cannot, never the video again: each step's cases with their traces, its interface, a worked example with real values, a diagram of how it works that you step through and click, the decisions in force and the rest of `plan.md`; on the Built side, What changed, each kind of change with its files and the runs that show it, worked examples to predict or toggle, and every choice the agent made. Select any words on it to leave a note, suggest an edit or ask: the same box as a mark on the video, sent with the same review. `guide --check`, in the build, stops a page that leaves out a heading or paragraph of `plan.md`, or shows a run as real that `runs/` does not hold.
- **The review server** — `reelplanning review`: serves the review page (every video in the repo: the system video, each plan's two videos, and each explainer as a row of its own, **Explainer**, saying the commit it explains, how many commits since, and what came of it: Done, or the plan it led to) and sends a notification when a video is ready, saying what it will ask ("2 choices to make, 3 min"). The Finish panel's Send gives it the review; it keeps it in the inbox and hands it on exactly once: to an open session waiting for it, else to the headless run, else it waits for the next session. A question asked on the page goes only to a session waiting on it (`review --wait`), which answers with `inbox answer`; it never starts the headless run.
- **The headless run** — the agent's command in `.reelplanning/config.json` (`claude … -p`), which the review server starts when no session is waiting. Nobody watches it, so it runs in auto mode (a classifier approves each action; anything that would ask is refused) inside the sandbox: its commands write only inside the repo, and a hook refuses a file-tool write outside it; only `git commit` runs outside the sandbox, so commits stay signed. Before starting one, the server checks that the sandbox works on this machine; if not, the run goes ahead with the sandbox off (its commands are no longer fenced to the repo; the hook still keeps its file writes there), and the reviewer is told. When the run ends, the server sends the notification: ready, or stopped, with its log.
- **The reel CLI** — `.reelplanning/` project management: init, new-plan, stage, check, record, audit, stops, status, memory, retro. `reel stops` names the calls that pause the walkthrough video: an off-plan change always, a call labelled visible or hard-to-undo, and a call sharing a label with a recent late fix; every other call goes on the list at the end. `reel check` holds a new plan to the rules: the owner's answers in force on the parts it touches, each cited or superseded with the reason; an accepted call of the agent's is history, which the check never asks for by part, and `reel check --base <commit>` names one only when the diff changes the lines its commits wrote (D-306). When a part's rules pile up, `reel fold <part>` drafts them as one section of this file for the owner to approve, and a plan then cites that section instead of each id. `reel audit` fails a walkthrough while a finding of the code check is unanswered, or a step has five calls and no question about it. `reel record` adds a review's answers to the decision log and files the review once, as `reviews/<kind>-<time>.json` with a short `.md` of what to act on, stamped with its reviewer and the reelplanning version, with the questions asked on the page listed to answer in the next version; a later review goes beside it, never over it. It also adds a summary of the review to the reviewer's own memory. An explainer's review is filed the same way, as `reviews/explainer-<time>` beside the explainer, by scene, with what the viewer wants next, and adds nothing to the decision log. `reel new-plan <repo> <slug> --from <explainer-dir>` starts a plan from it (Plan this): `plan.md` opens with "Explained first", titled by what was picked, and its problem quotes the review; `reel prereqs` lists the explainer first under Before you watch, so the plan video leans on it and starts at what changes (D-248). `reel status` ends with the repo's memory: at most five lines worked out each time from the ledger and the reviews (recommendations taken, own-words answers, rewinds, quick checks answered wrong, misses), with `reel memory <id>` for the evidence and `--you` for the reviewer's own, across repos; the plan-to-video skill reads it before writing a plan and when tagging calls. When one is due, it suggests a retro: `reel retro` starts a plan of skill edits the evidence supports.
- **The revise step** — acts on a plan review. Approved: each comment goes into its step in `plan.md` as text, and there is no new video. Changes requested: it rewrites only the steps a comment touched, rebuilds those beats and shows the video again.
- **The implement step** — implements `plan.md` with the decisions in force as constraints, and logs every call the plan did not cover into `walkthrough.md` as it makes it, tagged when a reviewer might overturn it (visible, hard to undo, a close call). Before any walkthrough video, a second, fresh agent checks the diff against the plan and the ledger from a brief `code-check` writes; `reel audit` fails until every finding is answered in `walkthrough.md`.
- **The walkthrough fix step** — acts on a walkthrough review: it changes the code for each flagged call, own-words answer or disagreed quick check, and updates the call's row. Accepted: the fixes are logged, with no new video. Changes requested: it also rebuilds just those beats and shows the walkthrough again. A flag that would overturn a ledger decision becomes a new plan instead.
- **The system video** — one video explaining the whole repo from this file, rebuilt where this file changes (`spec-diff` names the frames and what they cost to narrate). Lives in `.reelplanning/system-video/`. A review of it reaches the agent like any other; `system-review` files it and sorts each comment: the video is wrong (fix the frame), a small change inside one part (make it, shown in a one-step plan's walkthrough), or anything with a choice (a new plan).
- **The explainer** — a video of something already there, made when someone asks what is going on, before any plan: `reelplanning explain "<their question>" <source> …` pins the sources that hold the answer in `.reelplanning/explainers/<date>-<slug>/` (`explain.md`, `sources.json`, and `video/` started with `kind: explainer`). A source is pinned by its form (a path in the repo or outside it, a commit or a range, `worktree`, `since:<date>`, `pr:<n>`, `ci:<run-id>`, `this-session`, a decision) and described by its shape (a sequence, files, a table or text); a file outside the repo is kept by its path, hash and line count, never its text, and a source over about 200 lines gets a part of the guide. What it can be of is open (D-250): there is no list of kinds, only principles, the same for any of them: the question first, the shape before the detail, the real thing quoted, what moved, what is settled and open, 2–4 minutes. `build` runs `check-sources` on it: a quoted line its source does not hold word for word, an unpinned source, or a secret, an email address or a home path anywhere in its text stops the build until the text itself is fixed or masked (never blurred: the text is committed). It asks nothing to decide, and is a snapshot: never rebuilt because the repo moved; asked again, a new one is made.

## Pipelines

**The review loop** (`system.json` id `review-loop`):
1. A reviewer marks, comments and decides in the player, then presses Finish: approve, or request changes.
2. The review server keeps the review in the inbox and hands it to a waiting session, or starts the headless run.
3. `reel record` adds the answers to the decision log and files the review, with what to act on.
4. The revise step folds the comments into the plan (approved), or rewrites only the steps they touched (changes requested).
5. narrate voices only the changed lines, finish-project reassembles the video, and plan-diff flags what changed.
6. A notification says the video is ready, and the player plays just the changes.

Every step exists and is tested. A real system-video review went from Finish through the headless run to the notification in 9 min 13 s; a plan review has not been through a headless run yet. The sandbox was proven on a weaker setting than the one that ships, which is untested on a normal macOS or Linux machine.

**The build loop** (`system.json` id `build-loop`):
1. The implement step builds from the approved plan and logs every call the plan did not cover, tagging the ones a reviewer might overturn.
2. A second agent checks the diff against the plan and the ledger.
3. The walkthrough video shows each change running, before and after, with its guide under it. It pauses at each call `reel stops` names; the others are one list at the end, each with a Flag.
4. The reviewer accepts or requests changes; the review reaches the agent as in the review loop.
5. The walkthrough fix step changes what was flagged and, when changes were requested, rebuilds only those beats.
6. `spec.md` is updated and the system video rebuilds only the frames it affects.

All six exist and ran for real on the revise-loop plan: its reviewer answered call A18 and quick check k3 in their own words, and both became fixes. The stop rule and the grouped beat have their tests; no plan has yet been judged under them for ten reviews.

## Invariants

- **`decisions.md` is append-only.** A revise never edits a past ledger entry; it can only add one (via `reel record`, before revising) or mark one superseded, or reopened when the answer asked for the question again.
- **Only an answer is a decision.** "Explain this more" never enters the ledger; the question is explained better and asked again. An explainer asks no questions, so its review adds nothing to the ledger, whichever end is pressed; a comment on it reaches a decision only through a plan (Plan this). An own-words reply that turns out to ask for the same is recorded, then marked reopened by hand.
- **The code check is never the implementer.** The agent that wrote the code does not check it; the checker starts from the brief alone, and its findings are answered in the walkthrough, never fixed quietly.
- **A frame keeps its composition id across a revise.** `plan-diff` matches by id first — renaming or recreating a frame turns a real edit into a false add+remove, and the player's "only what changed" promise breaks.
- **The revise step only touches a step a comment, mark, flag or own-words answer actually landed on.** A plain option pick with no attached text needs no rewrite — the ledger already recorded it.
- **Plan history lives in git, not in parallel plan.md versions.** A revise edits `plan.md` in place and lands as a commit; `git log` is the record, not `reel new-plan`'s versioning.

## Several people

When a second person opens pull requests, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, the pull request template and `config.json`'s `maintainers` set up who decides. A pull request gets a walkthrough video when it changes over 300 lines outside tests, docs, videos and generated files, or the contributor ticks that it makes a choice you'd notice or can't easily undo; a smaller one lists its other choices in its text, one line each, for the maintainer to accept. The built videos never go into git: they are packed and pushed on a branch of their own, `video/pr-<n>`, which the maintainer clones and reviews with `reelplanning review`. The maintainer's review counts; a contributor's own review of their walkthrough adds nothing to the decision log. `reel pr-check` says where a pull request stands (and fails with `--merge` until it is ready), and `reel renumber` settles two branches' decision numbers after a merge.

## How it is tested

`npm test` runs the pipeline's specs and the player's. `reel case-study` runs one prompt three ways, a text plan, an HTML page and reelplanning, each to a finished site, and builds the page that sets them side by side.

## Knowledge levels

- `new`: nothing about reelplanning.
- `familiar`: the parts above, not which script calls which.
- `owner`: the pipeline order and why each invariant exists.

## Conventions

- Scripts live in `scripts/`, one file per pipeline step, invoked from the repo root.
- `.reelplanning/` templates live in `templates/reelplanning/`; anything scaffolded by `reel init` for a *reviewed* repo (including `config.json`, the headless command) belongs there, not hand-added once to this repo's own copy. `.reelplanning/inbox/` is machine-local and never committed; the reviewer's own memory is `~/.reelplanning/you.jsonl`, outside any repo.
- Detail page templates live in `templates/details/`, one self-contained file per kind with the comment bridge built in; a detail loads nothing from the network and nothing outside its video's `details/`.
- Tests: `npm test` runs the pipeline specs (`scripts/test/`: the ledger, record, reviews, memory, narration, the loop, the build, details) and the player's Playwright specs.
- Scripts run from the npm package (`npx -y reelplanning@<version> <script>`) with the user's repo as the working directory; they never write inside the package. In this repo, run this checkout's own `node bin/reelplanning.mjs` instead.

# Glossary

One name per thing, and no synonyms. The Term is the name in the files, commands and the decision log. Where a row has an **On screen** word, that is what a viewer sees and hears instead: narration, captions, frames and the player say *choice*, not call (D-127: plain words on screen, the files keep theirs). A plan's prose says the plain word too. Add a row when a plan introduces a part; never rename a row (add the old name under "also called", then stop using it). A row for a part that is gone is removed with it.

Every row is explained by a scene of the system video tagged `- defines: <term>`; a new row makes the system video behind until one is (`reel status`, `check-terms`).

The words under **Other words**, at the end, are the trade's own (repo, branch, merge, pull request): a meaning a viewer can open on any video, which the system video does not have to explain, and adding one never makes it behind. `check-terms` finds a word like these said or shown with no meaning anywhere (D-216); add its row there, or give it a meaning in the storyboard's `terms:` line. A phrase fresh eyes flagged, and the author kept, goes there too when other videos say it (D-226).
