# Memory: learn which questions are the right ones, from what reviews already record

## The problem

Every plan starts from nothing. The records say a lot about what you care about, but no plan reads
them. You took the recommended option on every question you answered from a list, and wrote your
own words on 3 of 16: the options were too abstract (D-022), the question was framed wrong (D-023),
or you accepted with a condition (D-003). You accepted 85 of 91 of the agent's calls. The steps you
rewound are the ones you later flagged or questioned, and one was rewound again after its revision.
Most of your own-words notes are questions. And some things got past a review and were changed
later: a decision superseded (D-056 by D-063), a call accepted and reversed after review (the
file tools, k3), a part reworked within days. Each of those is a question that should have been
asked, or a call that should have stopped.

The aim is the right questions, not fewer. The tool can also improve itself, which needs a
yardstick outside its own numbers: it could "improve" by asking less and counting that as success.

## What changes

Four changes; the first three stand alone, the fourth uses them.

1. **Every review says who and which skill** (step 1): the record needed to learn from it.
2. **Memory, worked out on demand** (steps 2 and 3): for this repo and for you across repos, a few
   lines up front with the evidence one command away, never a hand-kept file.
3. **Misses decide what stops** (step 4): something changed later that a review let through makes
   its kind of question or call stop next time.
4. **A retro you start** (step 5): the tool suggests one; you decide.

## Steps

### Step 1 — Every review records its reviewer and the skill version

*Independent.*

Intake adds the reviewer (git's `user.email` where the review is recorded, or the page's viewer) and
the reelplanning version that built the video to every review in `reviews/`. Old reviews are left as
they are. Without the version, a change to the skill can't be told apart from a change in the plans;
without the reviewer, one person's taste can't be told apart from what works.

### Step 2 — The repo's memory: `reel status` says what reviews show

*Needs step 1 (to tell reviewers apart; works without it for one reviewer).*

`reel status` ends with at most five lines, worked out each time from the ledger and `reviews/`:
how often a recommendation was taken; the questions answered in own words, and why; the steps
rewound, and those rewound again after a revision; the quick checks answered wrong; the misses (step
4). Each line has an id, and `reel memory <id>` prints its evidence: the reviews, the words, the
times. The skill says to read it before writing a plan and when tagging calls. Nothing is stored
that the reviews do not already hold.

### Step 3 — Your memory, across repos

*Needs step 1.*

When a review is recorded, a short summary of it (verdict, own-words answers and their reasons,
rewinds, wrong quick checks, the kinds of call flagged) is appended to a file of your own, outside
any repo (question 1). `reel memory --you` works out the same few lines across every repo you have
reviewed in: your taste, not this repo's facts. The skill reads both.

### Step 4 — Misses decide what stops

*Needs step 2.*

A miss is something a later plan, fix or system-video review changed that an earlier review let
through: a decision superseded, an accepted call later flagged or reversed, a part reworked soon
after. `reel memory` traces each to the question or call that passed it, and its kind (a call's
tags, a question's topic). A call of that kind stops the walkthrough again, whatever its streak of
accepts (D-084's rule gains this one exception), and the next plan that touches that part asks that
kind of question.

### Step 5 — A retro you start, checked against a fixed benchmark

*Needs steps 2–4.*

`reel status` suggests a retro when one is due (question 2). `reel retro` starts a plan: skill edits
the evidence supports, each citing it, and it must cut at least as many words as it adds. It is
reviewed like any plan. Before it is accepted, the benchmark (the Bob Dylan example, redone from the
same prompt, next to its text and HTML baselines) is run with the old and the new skill and
compared, so the retro is judged by something outside the loop's own numbers.

## Components touched

- **The reel CLI** — `status`, `memory`, `retro`; intake records reviewer and version (steps 1–5)
- **The plan-to-video skill** — reads memory before planning and tagging (steps 2–4)

## Open questions for the reviewer

1. **Where does your memory across repos live?** (step 3)
- **A · A file in your home folder.** `~/.reelplanning/you.jsonl`, one line per review; any agent
  reads it through `reel memory --you`. Stays on this machine.
- **B · Claude's own memory.** Claude Code's user memory holds it; nothing new to keep, but only
  Claude reads it.
- **C · Not yet.** The repo's memory first; yours waits for a later plan.
I recommend A: it works under any agent, and it is the same kind of record the repo keeps.

2. **When does the tool suggest a retro?** (step 5)
- **A · Every five plans, or when a signal repeats three times.** Whichever comes first.
- **B · Only when a signal repeats three times.** No schedule; evidence only.
- **C · Never; you ask for one.** `reel retro` exists, nothing suggests it.
I recommend A: a schedule catches slow drift that no single signal shows, and you still decide.

## Decisions in force

- **D-084** Tags and your record decide what stops; step 4 adds misses to it.
- **D-085** Less scaffolding: memory is worked out from records, not kept by hand.
- **D-083** Quick checks, and a wrong answer is a comment: one of memory's signals.
- **D-082** A run nobody is watching runs in auto mode, in the sandbox. Unchanged; memory is read by
  whoever writes the plan, a session or such a run.
- **D-024** Detail pages start from a template. Unchanged.

## Not in this plan

Sharing memory between people, or across machines. Learning the style of frames (colours, pace).
Anything that changes a plan without a review.
